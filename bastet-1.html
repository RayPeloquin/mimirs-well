BASTET-1 — Feline Spectral Augmentation System

Biological vision enhancement through external spectral translation.

Concept and proposed tests only. No hardware performance, feline safety, improved biological perception or animal communication is established. We do no harm.

Architecture

Cats already have rod-rich retinas, adaptable pupils and a reflective tapetum lucidum. They cannot see in absolute darkness. The proposed system converts additional spectral information into visible images, without modifying the animal's eyes.

Hardware concept

Low-light sensor

High-sensitivity CMOS imaging, with separate calibrated visible/NIR channels.

Thermal sensor

Passive 8–14 μm camera, calibrated for temperature contrast.

Processing unit

Single-board computer for registration, denoising, fusion and brightness control.

Optical bench

Artificial eye or camera, reference targets, calibrated photometer and display.

Four illustrative views

Synthetic drawings, not sensor captures, feline vision models or experimental results. Thermal colors are arbitrary display encodings.

Mathematical foundation

F(x,y,t) = wᵥV + wₙN + wₜT;   wᵥ + wₙ + wₜ = 1

V, N and T are normalized, spatially registered sensor channels; weights are nonnegative. Output is clipped to a separately established safe display limit: Lout = clip(gF, 0, Lmax). No numerical biological exposure limit is assumed.

Experimental validation

Hypothesis: calibrated spectral fusion improves object discrimination compared with conventional brightness enhancement at matched output luminance. If no improvement is observed, the proposed advantage is unsupported.

Mix That Meow — proposed acoustic branch

Pair BASTET with Temporal Acoustics to investigate whether feline vocalization patterns and non-invasive environmental measurements correlate. Use only lawfully obtained recordings or synthetic calls; no playback to animals is required. Compare spectrograms, fundamental frequency, duration, temporal modulation and call context against shuffled and held-out baselines. Account for recording conditions and individual variation. This is pattern analysis, not a claim of human language, supernatural identity or translation of animal thoughts.

Separate from the optical experiment; proposed pairing only, no registered equation coupling.

Animal welfare and evidence

Phase 1 is exclusively an artificial optical system. No healthy animal is to be fitted with hardware, restrained, exposed to lasers or injected. Any later observation using a stationary voluntarily accessible display would require independent veterinary and animal-welfare review, with avoidance and distress as stop signals. No direct biological application is authorized.

Scientific precedent: Ma et al., “Mammalian Near-Infrared Image Vision through Injectable and Self-Powered Retinal Nanoantennae,” Cell (2019), doi:10.1016/j.cell.2019.01.038. Mouse findings do not establish feline safety or effectiveness; the injection technique is specifically outside this design.
