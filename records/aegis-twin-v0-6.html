Summary

AEGIS-TWIN explores a bilateral slide-in protective exoskeleton: intact organic arms remain inside segmented powered sleeves, with a separate RESTORE pathway for missing or damaged limbs. Proposed CHROMATASKIN sensing, muscle-intent signals, joint-state estimation and feedback form an artificial nervous-system architecture. Gentle interaction and useful assistance are joint goals.

Evidence and limits

Stage-0 design brief and generated exterior/cutaway illustrations only. No prototype, validated protection, strength amplification, neural interface, sensory restoration, clinical performance, universal fit, or human-use testing is established. Published exoskeleton and sensory feedback research motivates the approach but does not validate AEGIS-TWIN. RESTORE may investigate existing clinical nerve–muscle interfaces, including AMI-type connections; Dave-specific compatibility is unknown.
