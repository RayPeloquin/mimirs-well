GRASP-01 — Grasp-recovery simulation benchmark

Record ID: grasp-01-grasp-recovery-simulation-benchmark | Expanded record

Expanded record · evidence type uncategorized

Standard public record; no technical-release approval implied. Publication policy

Evidence type describes the public artifact, not a validation or safety certificate.

Summary

An executable simulation benchmark for comparing grasp-recovery policies under the same seeded object properties and sensor/actuator faults. It contrasts fixed grip, reactive squeeze, and guarded recovery in a one-dimensional vertical pinch model. Task completion, controlled set-down, drops, and damage are scored separately so preservation is not mistaken for throughput.

Evidence and limits

The saved report embeds a seeded simulation manifest, aggregate outcomes, and selected recorded trajectories. There are 800 scenario instances per policy, or 2,400 policy episodes in total. Families: nominal, friction step, heavy payload, sensor dropout, force bias, slow actuator, fragility mismatch, and combined faults. Saved aggregate results: fixed: 550/800 complete; 10 controlled set-downs; 235 drops; 0 grip-damage events; 240 impact-damage events; 240 failures. reactive: 518/800 complete; 10 controlled set-downs; 126 drops; 164 grip-damage events; 126 impact-damage events; 272 failures. guarded: 385/800 complete; 231 controlled set-downs; 100 drops; 98 grip-damage events; 105 impact-damage events; 184 failures. Failure flags can overlap; drops, grip damage, and impact damage must not be summed as independent outcomes. Controlled set-down is not task completion. Guarded recovery completed 48.125% of episodes, versus 68.750% fixed and 64.750% reactive. Its drop rate was 12.500%, versus 29.375% fixed and 15.750% reactive. Its grip-damage rate was 12.250%, versus 0% fixed and 20.500% reactive. Guarded control therefore did not dominate both baselines on all metrics. The strict undamaged slip-settling diagnostic recorded zero successful recoveries for all three policies. Slipped episodes were 268 fixed, 261 reactive, and 137 guarded; all remained failed or censored under that diagnostic. Set-down counts must not be relabeled as successful slip recovery. Physics checks run at a modeled 500 Hz; control packets and replay frames are 50 Hz. The report gives pointwise Wilson intervals and 2,000 stratified paired bootstrap resamples for differences, without multiplicity correction. These describe this artificial suite, not real factory prevalence or hardware reliability. This website update inspected saved data and arithmetic; it did not rerun the Python benchmark.
