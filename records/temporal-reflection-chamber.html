Summary

A proposed temporal-reflection experiment using a rapidly switched transmission-line metamaterial inside a conductive enclosure. The approximately 8-foot cube is optional shielding/workspace infrastructure; the active temporal interface is along the line, not throughout the room air.

Evidence and limits

Published temporal-reflection experiments provide precedent for engineered transmission lines; they do not validate this proposed apparatus or a room-wide temporal interface. The archive reports numerical/algebraic checks by Wolfram Language and Python, not hardware simulation or experimental verification. Ideal geometry calculations for an 8-foot air-filled conducting cube give a lowest resonance of 86.936220 MHz and an air-crossing time of 8.133627 ns. Real construction changes losses and modes; air-crossing time is not a trigger-skew budget. In an ideal uniform nondispersive line with fixed inductance and capacitance multiplied by r, velocity, impedance and frequency magnitude scale as 1/sqrt(r). For r=4 the ideal backward-arrival relation is t_b=3t_s−2t_i at the same reference plane. These are conditional planning predictions; actual dispersion, switching, receiver and cable delays require calibration. Proposed evidence combines reversed feature order, duration and frequency scaling, arrival-time sweeps, asymmetric waveform repeats and source-off/static/bypass/boundary-switch controls. None of those proposed gates is reported as passed. Ordinary echoes, mixing and switching artifacts remain competing explanations. No stopped time, long-term broadcast retention, net energy gain or unusual conservation behavior is established.
