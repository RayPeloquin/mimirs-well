Summary

A small executable reference showing why permission must still be valid when a protected write occurs, rather than relying on an earlier preflight approval.

Evidence and limits

Rerun during this review: 17 functional checks and 200 controlled real-thread schedules passed; four deliberately broken variants were detected. All 100 revoke-first schedules refused the write with zero protected mutations; all 100 commit-first schedules retained the earlier authorized write. Rerun output exactly matched archived results. Events force the two orders; this is not exhaustive concurrency exploration. One process, one in-memory resource, trusted request context, a shared lock, a public test HMAC key and a local clock. No distributed-revocation, crash-durability, real identity-provider, tamper-resistant audit or remote-effects guarantee. Other write paths outside the model remain out of scope.
