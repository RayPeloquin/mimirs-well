Summary

A finite-reserve model for exploring transport, precipitation and pore filling in Roman-inspired self-healing concrete. Current work is an audit of illustrative numerical demos, not a validated material formulation or structural design.

Evidence and limits

The saved audit reports execution of two supplied demos unchanged. Neither implements a displacement/equilibrium solve, pressure-driven capillary flow or finite lime-calcium supply. Capacity check under the starter's own one-to-one reaction law: maximum porosity drop = volume per mole × available reaction capacity. At 3 × 10⁻⁵ m³/mol and 250 mol/m³, the limit is 0.0075; a crack starting at porosity 0.80 cannot fall below 0.7925 through that law alone. The demo instead uses a volume per mole 500 times larger, changing attainable sealing rather than merely accelerating time. A uniform interior-cell check in the first script creates 0.000599976 mol/m³ calcium in one two-second step, a 0.240% inventory error. Its open-crack fraction remains 0.089375 from step 50 through step 399, so that metric does not demonstrate closure. A periodic transport test also finds a nonzero net numerical divergence. The audit recommends conservative shared-face fluxes and direct updates of stored calcium. These are saved code-level diagnostics, not experiments. No material-calibrated healing time, recovered structural strength, repaired multiphysics solver or 2,000-year prediction is established.

Contributors

Independent numerical audit archived with Mr. Peloquin's research collection. Authorship of the two original scripts is not identified in the supplied files and remains unresolved. Scientific background: Linda M. Seymour and coauthors (2023), hot-mixing and lime-clast mechanisms; Marie D. Jackson and coauthors (2017), Roman marine-concrete mineral cements. PFLOTRAN developers provide the cited Richards-flow conservation framework. Their work does not endorse these scripts or this project.
