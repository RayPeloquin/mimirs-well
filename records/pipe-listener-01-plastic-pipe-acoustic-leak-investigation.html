PIPE-LISTENER-01 — Plastic-pipe acoustic leak investigation

Record ID: pipe-listener-01-plastic-pipe-acoustic-leak-investigation | Expanded record

Expanded record · evidence type uncategorized

Standard public record; no technical-release approval implied. Publication policy

Evidence type describes the public artifact, not a validation or safety certificate.

Summary

Research toward an acoustic listener for quiet cracks in plastic water-distribution pipes. The proposed system gathers additional evidence before recommending investigation and explicitly reports UNKNOWN when evidence is inadequate. The initial setting is utility distribution networks with acoustic access points and investigation records; building plumbing requires separate validation.

Evidence and limits

The saved brief reports a raw-data audit, derived spectra, a read-only evidence-admission module, analytic checks, and a prospective experiment. It reports 24 passing software/analytic checks. This website update reviewed the brief; it did not rerun the software or independently recompute the raw recordings. Reported Sheffield MDPE results at the known leak position, 2.8 bar, 200–800 Hz band: 3 mm valve/nozzle — 7.158 L/min and 221.55 mm/s² acceleration RMS; 3.6 mm direct hole — 9.234 L/min and 16.31 mm/s²; lengthwise slit — 9.204 L/min and 12.02 mm/s²; crosswise slit — 10.440 L/min and 15.71 mm/s². Flow values are attributed to the original authors; acceleration values are the brief's recomputation. In these recordings, the lengthwise slit lost approximately 1.286 times as much water as the valve/nozzle but had approximately 18.44 times lower acceleration in this band. This is a descriptive comparison from one rig, not a detection improvement, sensitivity ratio, or universal pipe property. The admission module returns UNKNOWN for stale evidence, installation/configuration changes, invalid signals, unverified sensor contact, or unverified application domain. It consumes diagnostic facts as inputs; it does not establish those facts itself. Coherence alone is not proof of a leak, and digitized silence cannot alone distinguish a quiet pipe from a detached sensor. Limits: no calibrated classifier, verified hardware contact diagnostic, measured power consumption, or demonstrated field superiority in v0.1. The Sheffield paired recordings lack simultaneous slit and no-leak/nuisance trials. Separate sensor placements are not treated as simultaneous pairs. Duplicate recordings are not independent controls; the Dongguan audit reports 1,000 clips but only 964 unique waveforms. Hong Kong raw recordings were not acquired for the reported work.
