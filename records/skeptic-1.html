Summary

Matter that asks a second question. SKEPTIC-1 proposes a bench instrument that measures a mechanical system, applies a verified reversible stiffness probe, measures again, and checks return to baseline. The aim is to distinguish explanations that passive frequency measurements cannot separate. The published software demonstrates a reduced numerical example, not a working diagnostic system.

Evidence and limits

Published ideal model: three 0.1 kg masses connected by four nominal 1000 N/m springs. A 25% stiffness loss in either end spring produces the same unbraced frequencies: 11.431406672, 21.798818369, and 29.165501437 Hz. Adding a 500 N/m grounded brace at mass 1 separates the first modes: left-end loss 12.750896532 Hz; right-end loss 12.348867760 Hz; gap 0.402028772 Hz. A symmetric center brace leaves the two faults ambiguous, providing a negative control. Stacking baseline and braced observations changes local sensitivity rank from 2 to 3 for left loss, right loss, and a uniform stiffness-scale nuisance. This is local identifiability within the selected three-parameter model, not proof of global identification. Committed results report passed healthy-chain closed-form, analytic-versus-finite-difference sensitivity, and center-probe ambiguity checks. The source also includes reflection, failed-brace, and return-drift checks. This website update checked published outputs; it did not rerun the Python model. Limits: hardware is unbuilt; the adaptive policy and decision thresholds are not implemented or validated. No measured noise floor, repeatability, accuracy, cost, safety benefit, or statistical detection rate. Damping, friction, fixture mass, actuator hysteresis, and sensor-error physics are omitted. Uniform stiffness scale is not a validated temperature model. Wrong-hypothesis searches are local multistart optimizations, not certified global exclusions.
