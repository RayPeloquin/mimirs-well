SILT HARP — Manual muddy-water prefiltration

Record ID: silt-harp-manual-muddy-water-prefiltration | Expanded record

Summary

A proposed power-free pre-treatment tool for muddy water, intended to reduce the solids burden before separate downstream treatment. SILT HARP is manually operated, cleaned, and emptied. Its output remains untreated feedwater; clearer appearance does not establish drinking-water safety.

Evidence and limits

Reviewed concept notes describe a coarse screen, gravity settling, a hand-operated wiper or brush, and a manual sludge dump. They exclude electronics, ultrasonic cleaning, and automatic backflushing. The reviewed records contain design descriptions and AI review commentary, not a measured performance report. No verified flow rate, screen size, turbidity-reduction curve, cleaning interval, service life, or field operating limit is established here. Numerical targets mentioned in historical notes remain unvalidated and are not published as capability ratings. The archive's internal approval language describes agreement on the concept; it is not independent engineering certification or evidence that hardware was built and qualified. The documented role is solids pre-treatment only. The reviewed material does not establish pathogen reduction, chemical contaminant removal, or potable-water performance. Downstream treatment and verification remain separate tasks.

Contributors

The reviewed reconciliation is signed Kimi and includes quoted Asteris/Court design statements. These establish archived AI-review participation, not independent experimental validation. Primary mechanical-design authorship and individual implementation contributions are not established by the reviewed excerpts and remain to be documented.
