GENTLE-01 — Low-force gripper

Record ID: gentle-01-low-force-gripper | Expanded record

Expanded record · evidence type uncategorized

Standard public record; no technical-release approval implied. Publication policy

Evidence type describes the public artifact, not a validation or safety certificate.

Summary

A proposed low-force, two-jaw robotic gripper with replaceable tactile fingertip cartridges and explicit grasp refusal. Its first intended task is short, low-height transfers of qualified thin-wall polymer packaging and inert test objects. It should hold only when a demonstrated force interval can resist slipping without exceeding the object-family damage limit, including uncertainty and stopping overshoot.

Evidence and limits

The brief contains a candidate architecture, illustrative force calculations and sensitivity plots, fault-handling requirements, and a proposed validation program. It states that no hardware was built or tested. No measured delicate-object performance, damage-free guarantee, affordability, or superiority is established. Candidate architecture: independently driven jaws, per-jaw output limiting, compliant instrumented fingertips, normal-load sensing, and optical slip cues. Optical sensing may request only bounded force changes; it is not an independent certified force limit. Pad identity and calibration must remain linked after replacement. All forces in the worked examples are per finger. For an illustrative 100 g object, friction lower bound 0.40 gives a required normal force of 1.933 N and a command interval of 2.033–2.650 N. At friction 0.25, the required force rises to 3.093 N and the command interval is empty: 3.193–2.650 N, so the model refuses the grasp. These numbers use assumed acceleration, load margin, force uncertainty, overshoot, and damage limit. They are not measured gripper specifications. The model does not qualify torsional slip, off-center loads, impacts, adhesion, changing contact area, or evolving material damage. Limiting resultant normal force does not establish a local pressure limit. Proposed first bench targets are 20–60 mm object width, 20–100 g mass, and at most 10 mm clearance above a padded catch surface. These are unqualified development targets. Full end-effector mass, inertia, center of mass, cabling, and allowed robot stop profiles still require integration assessment. The brief's reliability illustration states that zero failures in 100 independent, identically distributed trials gives a one-sided 95% upper failure-probability bound of approximately 2.951%. Repeated grasps on one specimen do not establish independence or broad reliability. No such prototype trial result is claimed.

Contributors

The brief records these Court-review contributions: Grok challenged force conventions and load paths; Claude narrowed independent-drive and fault policy; Gemini checked arithmetic and statistical limits; Meta emphasized service effort and a matched baseline. AI critique informed the concept but is not independent engineering validation. Individual primary-design authorship is not explicitly identified in the reviewed brief.
