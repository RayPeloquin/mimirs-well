Summary

Temporal Acoustics explores whether non-invasive wave measurements can identify present-day voids within unknown material, with a longer-term research question about how underground structures may have changed. The current implementation is a controlled computational inverse-imaging pilot, not an archaeological survey or proof of ancient events.

Evidence and limits

2D scalar finite-difference wave propagation with a circular pressure-release void surrogate, five synthetic source positions, thirteen receivers along one depth, a known 900 Hz pulse, effective-speed calibration and diffraction-style migration. This is not coupled elastic P/S-wave tomography. Eight randomized synthetic rock worlds, with two independent noisy acquisitions per class, produced 16 cavity-present and 16 cavity-absent cases. A held-out-world threshold trained on smoother no-cavity controls gave 100% sensitivity and 12.5% false-positive rate for the calibrated global-peak score in this small simulated sample. This is not field performance, and the peak is not a calibrated probability. Eight additional negative controls with sharp 35%-slower solid inclusions were deliberately outside the smooth-null calibration distribution. The calibrated interior-peak rule falsely flagged five of eight inclusions as cavities. Median location error for planted cavities across the varied worlds remained approximately 1.00 m. An 11%-faster deep synthetic lens and uniform rock produced the same short-offset calibrated speed (~2542 m/s), showing the inability of this direct-arrival geometry to constrain deep rock properties. Ten unit tests passed; reproducible source, exact inputs, metrics, and plotted controls are linked. No actual measurements of Malta, pyramids, cart ruts, caves or historical buildings were used.
