The Traveler

A civilian solar-electric airship concept designed to ride stratospheric winds around the Earth and return within reach of a staffed recovery site.

Airship and camera

The proposed 60–90-day mission would use stratospheric winds for most of the travel. Solar-electric propulsion would support limited corrections, with energy reserved for recovery. A gyroscope-stabilized camera frame would keep the view steady as the hull moves.

Historical wind replay

We ran 12 passive-drift scenarios using 2024 NOAA NCEP/NCAR Reanalysis 1 winds. Four seasonal starts at 42° N, 71° W were replayed at each of three pressure levels, for up to 90 days. The model scored a lap after 360° of net longitude change. It counted an approach only after the lap and within 500 km of the start at float altitude.

At the finest time step, the three approaches occurred on days 34.3, 41.6 and 76.8. The closest was 179 km away. Lap and approach classifications held across time steps from 60 to 7.5 minutes. Both runs stopped at the polar cutoff were inconclusive.

Evidence limits. This one-year replay models passive drift on pressure surfaces. Descent, physical recovery and airspace authorization remain open. The illustrations show a proposed design, and no aircraft or camera hardware was flown.
