01 / The three questions

Keep intent, evidence and hypothesis apart.

01 / INTENDED

What we intended

Study an external diaphragm-module approach to neonatal breathing support, beginning with artificial-lung evaluation rather than contact with a child.

A goal is not an experimental result.

02 / DOCUMENTED

What actually happened

The reviewed design abandoned an earlier magnetic lung-contact sleeve and rejected the assumption that a passive or limp state after power failure is necessarily safe. No device, artificial-lung benchmark, expiration measurement or clinical validation is reported.

This describes the available source record; no new tests are implied.

03 / POSSIBLE CROSSOVER

What else might this be useful for?

Independent verification of what happens after an actuator stops could become a useful generic failure-state test for assistive fluid systems. It must be tested on appropriate nonclinical rigs.

New hypothesis only; independent validation needed.

Evidence boundary: No prototype or clinical performance. An open path after shutdown is not assumed to provide safe breathing support.
02 / Next reproducible brick

What would change the record?

1 · Preserve

Define a synthetic-lung operating envelope and baseline.

2 · Measure

Measure displacement, expiration, backpressure and support continuity independently.

3 · Challenge

Preserve specialist medical and engineering review as prerequisites for any patient-facing claim.
