# Chihuahua Sphere / CS-1

**Status:** v0.1 calculation-only design; no apparatus built or tested.

## Purpose
A small, reconfigurable optical-collection laboratory inspired by large-scale energy-collection geometries. Adjustable panels and swappable receiver surfaces would let researchers compare geometry, source direction, electrical mismatch and heat in a **complete energy ledger**.

## Intended experiment
Start with an ordinary low-voltage light source and a characterized receiver. Compare each panel configuration with a fixed baseline over the same time interval. Record energies in joules.

| Measurement boundary | Energy entering | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lamp and driver | Electrical energy supplied | Light emitted, heat released, and change in stored energy |
| Receiver | Light incident on the panel | Electrical energy delivered to the load, light reflected or transmitted, heat released, and change in stored energy |

Light travels between the two boundaries. Measure the share reaching the receiver separately from the share that escapes or is absorbed elsewhere. For the whole apparatus, calculate electrical efficiency as energy delivered to the load divided by total electrical energy supplied, including any panel actuators used during the run.

For example, if 10 J reaches the receiver and 2 J reaches the load, the receiver converts 20% of its incident light into delivered electricity. The remaining 8 J must be accounted for at the receiver. The 10 J is an intermediate measurement and counts only once. Report any unexplained residual alongside measurement uncertainty. Future thermal-emitter studies would be separate projects.

## Evidence limits
No experimental collection efficiency, novel energy source, validated power gain or full spherical instrument. The proposed geometry is an instrument for comparing losses; it cannot violate conservation of energy.

## Next check
Calibrate one source and one receiver, measure alignment sensitivity and repeatability, and only then assess whether moving the panels offers an advantage over a simpler arrangement.
