# BRIDLE-2 — Modular aerospike comparison

**Status:** AI-assisted concept study v0.1; uncalibrated toy model, no hardware.

## Public purpose

BRIDLE-2 tests a design contrast with BRIDLE-1: replace portions of a common thrust-body concept with separately maintainable modules while retaining awareness of shared plume, cooling and frame effects. The important question is not whether modularity looks convenient, but whether fault response and serviceability justify additional seams, weight and interfaces.

The archived mathematical comparison deliberately produces a warning: under one illustrative local cooling impairment, a separated region can become *hotter* even while neighboring regions look better. This is a counterexample to assuming that modularity automatically makes thermal protection safer.

## Evidence limits

No manufactured module, flight or firing test, calibrated temperature prediction, reliable cooling margin, measured thrust, lifetime, structural qualification or safer-shutdown demonstration is established. The cited normalized values belong to a simplified model that omits many physical effects. The image and model cannot support an operating recipe.

## Next check

Define a fair, equal-resource comparison with BRIDLE-1 covering inspectability, local thermal behavior, shared failures and repair cost. Resolve the conceptual trade-off with qualified engineering review before considering any physical test.
