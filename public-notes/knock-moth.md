# Knock-Moth — Pool-tile mapping

**Status:** Private-source device concept; public purpose-level summary only. No demonstrated instrument.

## Public purpose

Knock-Moth explores whether consistent, operator-guided acoustic observations could help a mason find *changes* in the response of existing pool tile. The proposed tool is an inspection aid: a human selects comparison areas and decides what deserves closer examination. The archived proposal draws on the familiar trade practice of listening for differences when sounding a surface.

It does not equate a changed sound with a proven void, crack or structural defect. Backing materials, tile joints and surface conditions can alter the response. The source is marked private, so this digest deliberately omits its component layout, dimensions, electrical design and decision-control details.

## Evidence limits

No manufactured Knock-Moth, calibrated acoustic map, false-positive rate, minimum detectable defect, field comparison or independent inspection approval is established. A source brief and concept artwork describe an idea, not a verified diagnosis. Established hammer sounding is background practice, not validation of this particular tool.

## Next check

Have a qualified pool or masonry inspector specify the useful decision and a blinded, repeatable comparison against ordinary manual inspection. Establish how uncertain or inconsistent readings would be displayed before any defect claim.
