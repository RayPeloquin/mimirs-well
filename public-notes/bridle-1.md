# BRIDLE-1 — Monolithic cellular aerospike

**Status:** Propulsion architecture study v0.1; concept only, unmeasured.

## Public purpose

BRIDLE-1 investigates a single additively manufactured thrust-body concept containing multiple functionally separated combustion regions around an aerospike geometry. The research question is whether thermal-margin-aware control and maintained cooling after shutdown could improve fault handling, while honestly counting the extra system complexity.

Its archived study makes a useful distinction: separately controlled cells do not automatically provide independent protection. Shared structure, supplies and thermal paths remain possible common failure points. The proposed architecture is assessed against existing aerospike and additive-manufacturing precedent.

## Evidence limits

No fabricated thrust body, verified cooling network, hot-fire result, measured thrust, specific impulse, lifetime, redundancy, or novel thermodynamic cycle is established. Illustrative layout diagrams are not manufacturing CAD. Parts of the design concern energetic propulsion, so this public summary excludes implementation details and operating conditions.

## Next check

Have qualified specialists review manufacturability, inspection access and thermal common-cause failures at a non-operational research level. Compare proposed safety benefit against simpler cooling and shutdown baselines before any hardware program.
