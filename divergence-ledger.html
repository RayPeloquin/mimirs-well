01 / One project, three questions

KA PATCH v1.0

Paired front-and-back energy-harvesting patch concept · Small movements, small currents, new possibilities.

01 / INTENDED

What we intended

Explore whether thin, flexible patches on the chest and upper back can recover small amounts of electrical energy from respiratory and heartbeat-related movement, contact deformation, and temperature gradients.

Proposed method: piezoelectric, triboelectric, and thermoelectric layers, followed by power conditioning and an energy buffer.

02 / OBSERVED

What actually happened

A concept and its illustrations were developed. This public ledger has no measured output, prototype test, independent replication, or validated power budget to report.

Current outcome: unmeasured — neither technical success nor failure established. An illustration is not an experimental result.

03 / POSSIBLE CROSSOVER

What else might this be useful for?

Even if practical energy harvesting proves inefficient, flexible motion-sensitive layers might be worth testing for breathing-motion sensing, pressure mapping, or assistive-wearable input signals.

These are new hypotheses, not accidental discoveries or established applications.

The honesty gate: There is no recorded failure to reinterpret yet. This pilot shows how a proposed design can preserve promising alternative directions without fabricating evidence.
02 / The next reproducible brick

Measure first. Interpret second.

A small benchtop experiment can produce the first genuine observation for this ledger, without applying untested electronics to anyone's chest.

1 · Isolate

Test one candidate transducer with a known deformation input. Record frequency, displacement, load, and controls.

2 · Measure

Capture voltage and current under a defined electrical load; estimate harvested energy, then subtract power-conditioning losses.

3 · Reframe

If useful power is too low, examine whether the measured signal is repeatable enough for sensing. Label that result separately.

Before any wearable or medical use: assess electrical isolation, heat, adhesive and skin safety, repeatability, and compatibility with existing medical devices. No clinical utility is claimed.

How to use a Divergence Record

Record the original hypothesis before the trial. Keep raw results and test conditions separate from interpretation. Mark unknowns explicitly. If a different application emerges, describe it as a hypothesis until it is independently tested. Preserve previous versions so the next researcher can see exactly when the interpretation changed.

What would change this record from “unmeasured” to “tested”?

A date-stamped protocol, apparatus configuration, control runs, raw measurements, analysis method, and a stated result. A failed test belongs in the ledger just as much as a successful one; neither should be invented to fill a blank.
