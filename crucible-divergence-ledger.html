01 / The three questions

CRUCIBLE · Divergence Record

01 / INTENDED

What we intended

Test explicit mission contracts on simulated rover and quadrotor dynamics, preserve counterexamples, compare a deliberately defective baseline with a manually improved controller and verify replay integrity.

The original v0.2 campaign has 40 held-out scenarios per model for two controllers: 160 paired-controller evaluations.

02 / OBSERVED

What actually happened

The rover's guarded controller reduced held-out contract violations from 8/40 to 0/40. The quadrotor changed from 31/40 to 27/40—and included one regression. Later replay patches fixed a case where trace checksum alone could conceal changed verdicts, and rejected a changed physics timestep during checkpoint restore.

The saved campaign belongs to the historical 34-check ZIP. The current source has 37 validation checks; those patched revisions did not rerun or recertify the historical 160-trace campaign.

03 / CROSSOVER

What else might this be useful for?

The transferable method is an evidence-preserving failure laboratory for other software and cyber-physical systems: freeze source and assumptions, store counterexamples, test replay integrity, and explicitly retain regressions.

An engineering research direction, not production autonomy certification.

The divergence: An improved aggregate result can conceal an individual regression. Replaying the wrong environment can create false confidence. The full history is part of the safety evidence.

Check the 8/40 result

After the simulated operator link went down, the rover had 2.6 seconds to slow to 0.2 m/s or less and complete 0.4 seconds at that speed or below. A run counted as one violation if it missed that requirement or breached a speed, tilt or travel limit. The quadrotor had its own hold-position and safety requirements.

Each row pairs two controllers on the same held-out scenario. Count “Violation” in each column. For the rover, eight baseline violations became passes and the other 32 cases passed with both controllers. For the quadrotor, five cases improved and one regressed, taking the total from 31 violations to 27.

03 / Next reproducible brick

How to test the next question

1 · Preserve the original

Keep the release archive and source/environment hashes for the original held-out results.

2 · Challenge an independent controller

Test fresh, unseen faults; inspect the one quadrotor regression and prioritize mission-owner-approved requirements.

3 · Progress through validation gates

Calibrate physical and sensor models before SITL/HIL and controlled hardware trials. Use fresh held-out scenarios for any changed controller.

What should a later researcher not infer?

The independently coded Boolean oracle and quantitative monitor agreed in all original campaign runs, but share mission specifications and model assumptions. Agreement and exact replay cannot certify a real vehicle.
