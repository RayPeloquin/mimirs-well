01 / One study, three questions

The SKEPTIC-1 record

An idealized three-mass, four-spring experiment asks whether an asymmetric probe can disambiguate two fault locations that produce identical ordinary vibration spectra.

01 / INTENDED

What we intended

Design a bench instrument that measures a system, changes its stiffness in a known and reversible way, measures again, and checks that the original state returns.

Goal: distinguish competing mechanical explanations while explicitly detecting calibration failure, ambiguity, or return-state drift.

02 / COMPUTED

What actually happened

In the saved ideal numerical model, a 25% loss of the left-end or right-end spring gives exactly the same unbraced three-mode spectrum. A 500 N/m grounded brace at mass 1 breaks that equality: the first-mode predictions differ by 0.402028772 Hz.

Local parameter-sensitivity rank changes from 2 to 3 when baseline and braced observations are combined. A symmetric center brace remains ambiguous.

03 / POSSIBLE CROSSOVER

What else might this be useful for?

The negative control suggests a general experimental lesson: adding a measurement is not enough if the intervention preserves the system's symmetry. Deliberately chosen probes may be worth exploring in fixture inspection, robotic mechanism diagnostics, and experimental sensor placement.

Proposed transfer directions, not validated applications. Symmetry-breaking and experiment design have substantial prior art.

The divergence: “Probe it again” is not automatically informative. The location and symmetry of the probe matter. The failed center-brace control is an informative model result, not a failed physical prototype.

03 / The next experimental brick

Prove the intervention is real.

A useful next phase is not simply a more complicated fit. It is a controlled benchtop comparison against simpler ways of obtaining the same information.

1 · Build and calibrate

Measure actual stiffness, mass, actuator repeatability, sensor response, and return-state drift for a small laboratory test article.

2 · Blind the comparison

Compare adaptive bracing with fixed, random, and repeated passive tests, plus richer passive mode-shape or response measurements under matched resources.

3 · Keep adverse findings

Record when added stiffness fails to resolve ambiguity, when drift invalidates a test, or when passive methods work just as well.

Limits: No hardware has been built. Damping, friction, mounting, real sensor noise, and actuator hysteresis are omitted from the saved ideal model. Multistart fitting is a local search, not a global proof. Practical diagnostic utility and novelty remain unestablished.

Why is the center-brace result worth keeping?

The center brace leaves the model reflection-symmetric. A change to the experiment therefore produces no additional information about which end spring was weakened. This is a constructive negative control: it demonstrates why intervention design matters.

Does this establish a new diagnostic method?

No. Boundary-condition changes, added-stiffness identification, symmetry-breaking, and optimal experiment design have established research histories. The numerical example supports this particular ideal-model illustration, not priority, general diagnostic accuracy, or patent novelty.

What should the next researcher check?

Reproduce the existing source calculation, introduce realistic damping and sensor noise, pre-register a comparison between asymmetric, symmetric, random, fixed and passive measurements, and retain failed calibration and drift cases as first-class evidence.
