01 / INTENDED

What we intended

Build an on/off counting experiment and a traceable review protocol that tests whether apparent anomalies survive correct background treatment and multiple-trial accounting.

The published simulation evaluates 20,000 null families with 100 trials per family under explicit Poisson and exposure assumptions.

02 / OBSERVED

What actually happened

In saved null simulations, the family false-alert rate was 0.59% using Bonferroni correction with known background and 0.105% using a conditional exact on/off approach. A naïve estimated-background variant incorrectly alerted on 91.695% of null families. An unflagged rate-shift stress test also produced 32.445% false alerts.

All numbers are from synthetic assumptions and the saved experiment. The current software checkout has 15 passing protocol tests; older artwork and archives list fewer.

03 / CROSSOVER

What else might this be useful for?

The protocol may be useful in generic anomaly-detection pipelines: record exposure, provenance, trust in background estimates, blinded thresholds, replay controls, and the difference between an anomaly and a validated discovery.

Possible methodology reuse in sensors and scientific monitoring; no neutrino detection claim.

The divergence: A tiny p-value is not permission to ignore a broken background model. Better statistical bookkeeping can matter more than a more elaborate detector.

03 / Next reproducible brick

How to test the next question

1 · Re-run the reference

Repeat the saved experiment with pinned requirements and inspect the preserved seeds, rates and denominators.

2 · Stress assumptions

Vary exposure, count dispersion, hidden rate drift, metadata quality and correlated windows, logging every family.

3 · Separate software from sensors

Only after a calibrated physical detector exists could the analysis be tested on instrument data, against an independently specified background.

What should a later researcher not infer?

The null case reveals the central lesson: correcting for many trials is insufficient if the marginal background probabilities are poorly modeled. The saved experiment also notes that flags can suppress known problems but an unknown rate shift can still invalidate inference.
