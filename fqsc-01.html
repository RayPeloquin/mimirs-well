FQSC-01 Field-Quench Spectral Coupling

Where does the energy actually go?

The slingshot analogy asks a serious question: if electromagnetic energy is stored around a localized particle and the field changes, how much becomes internal excitation, how much becomes motion, and how much remains elsewhere in the system?

v0.1 · Concept artwork and computational proposal · Not built

01 / Proposed architecture

A controlled question in a compact instrument.

Confinement

Trapped-ion chamber

A conceptual sealed observation region for one or a few confined atomic ions. The physical trap technology and any eventual laboratory feasibility are separate specialist-engineering questions.

Field interaction

Controlled field history

A modelled time-varying electromagnetic interaction with an explicitly specified spectrum, coupling, duration and energy budget. A macroscopic switch is not assumed to generate nuclear-scale photons.

Reference and sensing

Optical state readout

Known atomic transitions supply a comparison for internal-state response; separate motional observables distinguish excitation from simple trap displacement or heating.

Measurement

Digital twin and analysis

Time-dependent calculations estimate transition probability, ion motion and remaining field energy. The displayed traces are illustrative, not measured data.

02 / The physical test

Energy stored is not energy absorbed.

For a weak electric-dipole transition, excitation depends on the transition matrix element and the field's spectral amplitude at the transition frequency, not just the total energy stored in the apparatus.

P(i → f) ∝ |⟨f|d|i⟩|² · |Ẽ(ωfi)|²

Computational test sequence

  1. Define a confined-ion model and prescribed field history, including a baseline without the proposed modulation.
  2. Calculate final internal-state populations and centre-of-mass motional energy separately.
  3. Track the energy budget and compare numerical predictions against standard weak-field perturbation theory.
  4. State uncertainty, sensitivity and conditions that would rule out any claimed advantage.
Physics boundary. Photon–nucleus interactions are established, but FQSC-01 does not demonstrate nuclear breakup or replace the Large Hadron Collider. Neither a large energy store nor a dramatic field change automatically produces a high-energy quantum.
03 / The Court's review

The narrowed idea survived. The shortcut did not.

Four responses from AI-assisted critical review converged on the spectral-coupling objection. Three called for revision and one recommended holding the nuclear-scale claims. The review narrowed the next question to atomic-scale modelling. The public record documents the group findings. Experimental validation remains open.

Related established work includes NIST's trapped-ion research and CERN's electromagnetic-dissociation measurements. These are prior art and context, not evidence that the proposed instrument works.
